# Review 01 — Log in with Google

## 1. Verdict

**Needs Changes**

| Severity | Count |
|----------|-------|
| Blocker | 0 |
| Major | 1 |
| Minor | 5 |

The spec is a faithful restatement of its source: every acceptance criterion and edge-case scenario is reproduced with its meaning intact, every one is covered in a normative section, the traceability matrix is complete and consistent, and the author was disciplined about gaps — PKCE, token validation, scope minimisation, and the `auth_identities` column list were all routed to Assumptions rather than tagged to acceptance criteria they do not derive from. What drives the verdict is **F-1**: the source story is an unratified, assistant-generated artifact. Its frame is explicitly marked "proposed, confirm", nine of its open questions are unanswered, fifteen of its assumptions are unconfirmed, and the spec itself records that OQ-1 may rewrite AC-3. A spec can only be as solid as the contract under it, and this contract is not yet agreed. The five Minor findings (F-2 through F-6) are ordinary spec-tightening items and would not, on their own, hold up development.

Source was obtained and AC fidelity was checked line by line (`source_verified: true`).

## 2. Findings

### F-1 · Major · D1 AC fidelity

**Location:** Front matter (`source: docs/stories/google-login.md`); §1; §6 A-1; §7 OQ-1

**Evidence:**
> A-1 | The "As a / I want / So that" statement in §1 is the source's proposed reconstruction of a one-line story, not a stakeholder-agreed frame.

> OQ-1 | Is the AC-3 posture correct — require the existing account password before linking Google to a matching email — or should the app trust Google's verified email and link automatically? … Blocks: AC-3 (the AC may be rewritten)

**Problem:** The source (`docs/stories/google-login.md`, front matter `author: story-clarifier`) was generated by an assistant from a one-line request. It carries a `[PROPOSED — confirm]` frame, nine open questions with no recorded answers, and fifteen `[ASSUMPTION — not confirmed]` items. The spec traces to it accurately, but the story has never been ratified by a product owner. Four of the seven acceptance criteria (AC-1, AC-3 scope, provider-agnostic scope, AC-5/AC-6 unlink behaviour) reflect choices made during a clarification exchange; AC-2, AC-4, AC-7 and all seven edge-case scenarios were composed without stakeholder input, and OQ-1 reopens the very AC-3 decision that was made.

**Why it matters:** Downstream, nobody can distinguish an acceptance criterion a stakeholder agreed to from one the tooling composed. If this spec goes to implementation planning, AC-3 gets built as written — a password challenge before linking — while OQ-1 says that may be the wrong posture and the alternative (trust Google's verified email) is a materially different build. The same risk applies in smaller form to every unconfirmed edge case.

**Resolves when:** A product owner signs off the source story — its frame, its seven acceptance criteria, and its seven edge-case scenarios — or the unratified items are marked as such in the spec so a reader knows which criteria are provisional. At minimum, OQ-1 is answered before the spec is treated as buildable.

### F-2 · Minor · D1 AC fidelity

**Location:** §2.1 and §2.2 (both AC and EC tables)

**Evidence:**
> AC-1 | **New user signs up with Google (just-in-time provisioning).** Given I am not signed in and no account exists for the email on my Google profile, when I select "Continue with Google" and approve the Google consent screen, then a new account is created … and I am signed in with the same session type a password login would produce, and I land on the same post-login destination as a password login.

**Problem:** The source states each acceptance criterion and edge case as a `gherkin` block with discrete `Given` / `When` / `Then` / `And` lines. §2 flattens each into a single comma-joined prose sentence. The words are preserved, but a reviewer can no longer diff the spec's criteria line-by-line against the story, which is the check §2 exists to support.

**Why it matters:** Structural drift hides wording drift. When the story is revised, a line added or removed inside a `gherkin` block is obvious in a diff; the same change buried in a prose sentence is easy to miss, and §2 is supposed to be the section a reviewer trusts to match the ticket exactly.

**Resolves when:** §2 reproduces each criterion in the same `Given-When-Then` block form the source uses.

### F-3 · Minor · D3 Grounding

**Location:** §3.2, fourth bullet

**Evidence:**
> - establishes a session of the same type and with the same properties a password login produces; `[AC-1]`

**Problem:** AC-1 says only "the same session type a password login would produce". "and with the same properties" is not in AC-1 — it restates A-10 ("the same lifetime, cookie flags, and revocation behavior"). The sentence is tagged `[AC-1]`, which presents A-10's content as if the acceptance criterion required it.

**Why it matters:** A tag is what stops the next reader from re-checking a statement against its AC. Folding an unconfirmed assumption into an AC-tagged sentence makes "same lifetime, cookie flags, revocation behaviour" look agreed when it is A-10 — a position the author flagged as disputable.

**Resolves when:** The normative sentence claims only "the same session type `[AC-1]`", and the broader session-properties claim stays in A-10, or A-10 is confirmed and promoted with its own basis.

---

### F-4 · Minor · D5 Testability

**Location:** §3.4 (AC-3 path); §2.1 AC-3

**Evidence:**
> When a user completes Google authentication, the identity is not yet linked, and the Google profile email matches an existing account that has a password and no linked Google identity, the app: does not sign the user in yet …

**Problem:** AC-3 turns on "the Google profile email matches an existing account". Neither the spec nor the source defines what "matches" means — case sensitivity, surrounding whitespace, Gmail dot-and-plus normalisation. A tester handed `User@Example.com` from Google and a `user@example.com` password account cannot say from the spec whether AC-3 fires.

**Why it matters:** Email match is the trigger for the entire linking flow and its security challenge. If the implementation normalises and the test doesn't (or vice versa), AC-3 passes in QA and fails for real users, or links accounts that shouldn't be linked.

**Resolves when:** The spec states the comparison rule for the email match (e.g. exact, or case-insensitive on a normalised address), or records it as an open question against AC-3.

---

### F-5 · Minor · D5 Testability

**Location:** §3.4 (AC-3) and §3.5 (EC-4); §2 AC-3, EC-4

**Evidence:**
> §3.4: … the Google profile email matches an existing account that has a password and no linked Google identity …
> §3.5: An attempt to link a Google identity that is already linked to another account is refused … `[EC-4]`

**Problem:** Both conditions can hold for one sign-in: the Google email matches an existing password account (AC-3 → prompt for that account's password), while the same Google identity is already linked to a third account (EC-4 → refuse). The spec does not say which path wins.

**Why it matters:** This is a real state — a person with two of the app's accounts and one Google account. Left unspecified, two developers implement two different resolutions and a tester has no expected result.

**Resolves when:** The spec states the precedence between the AC-3 and EC-4 conditions, or records it as an open question.

---

### F-6 · Minor · D5 Testability

**Location:** §3.7, second bullet; §2.2 EC-2

**Evidence:**
> - If Google returns no email, or an email it reports as unverified, sign-in does not complete, the user is shown a message that a verified email is required, and no account is created. `[EC-2]`

**Problem:** EC-2 is written entirely around the account-creation path — its outcome is "no account is created". The linking path (AC-4: an already-signed-in user connecting Google) has no account to create; the spec does not say what happens there when Google returns an unverified or absent email.

**Why it matters:** AC-4 is in scope and reachable with an unverified Google email. Without a defined outcome, the link might silently succeed with an unverified address, undermining the same guarantee EC-2 protects on the sign-up path.

**Resolves when:** §3.7 (or §3.5) states the behaviour for an unverified/absent Google email on the AC-4 linking path, or an open question records the gap.

## 5. Ambiguities and Missing Edge Cases

- **Email-match semantics for AC-3** — "the Google profile email matches an existing account" has no defined comparison rule (case, whitespace, alias normalisation). A tester cannot determine whether AC-3 fires for `User@Example.com` vs `user@example.com`. `[AC-3]` (F-4)
- **AC-3 and EC-4 both applicable** — Google email matches a password account *and* the Google identity is already linked elsewhere. No precedence stated. `[AC-3]` `[EC-4]` (F-5)
- **Unverified Google email on the linking path** — EC-2's outcome ("no account is created") does not fit AC-4, where the user is already signed in. Behaviour undefined. `[AC-4]` `[EC-2]` (F-6)
- **Post-login destination with no prior context** — AC-1 promises "the same post-login destination as a password login"; if that destination is normally "return to the page you were on", the value for a fresh Google sign-up with no prior page is undefined. Not raised as a numbered finding — low impact and a sensible default is obvious — but noted. `[AC-1]`
